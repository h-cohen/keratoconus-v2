1. Task, label and cohort

Task. Given one Pentacam examination of a keratoconic eye, estimate the probability that the eye will be referred for corneal cross-linking. The prediction is per scan. The clinical claim is for the first visit, i.e. the earliest scan of each eye in the cohort.

Label. y = 1 if the eye was cross-linked at any time according to the clinic's treatment sheet. Positive scans are those taken before the CXL date. Scans taken after an eye's CXL are excluded from the cohort. Positive scans fall 0–6 months before CXL (91), 6–12 months (41), 12–24 months (24) and more than 24 months (11).

Cohort. {n} scans, {n_eye} eyes, {n_pat} patients, {pos} positive scans. First visit: {n_first} eyes, {pos_first} positive. Age {age_mean} ± {age_sd} years (range {age_min}–{age_max}). Inclusion follows the original study: keratoconus, complete numerics, a four-map composite image, and front mean keratometry Km F ≤ 55 D. Scan dates are parsed from the image file, because the export's own date field has day/month swaps. Patients are keyed by a pseudonymous hash. No identifying fields are used.

2. Two arms: noH (primary) and H (auxiliary)

Every analysis is run and reported for both arms, side by side. They differ in one input block:

  • noH: single visit (primary result). Only what the Pentacam measures at the visit: both eyes' numerics and maps, plus age.
  • H: visit + fellow-eye treatment history (auxiliary). noH plus block H, which records whether the other eye was already treated before the visit date (§8). H is clinical history taken from the treatment sheet, not the scanner, so the H arm is labelled "visit + fellow-eye treatment history".

3. Pipeline overview

1 · VisitScanned eye: 55 numerics (incl. age) and a four-map composite. Fellow eye: same-day exam.
2 · Decode mapsColour → physical units (µm, D) on a 145×145 grid. Left eyes are mirrored.
3 · FeaturesN (205), R (96 × own / fellow / own−fellow = 288), 2 image flags; [+ H (5)].
4 · In-fold prepFit R standardisation, clip at the 0.5/99.5% quantiles, median imputation.
5 · Three learnersXGBoost, LightGBM (5-seed bag), elastic-net LR; each tuned by inner patient CV.
6 · BlendEqual-weight mean of the members' percentile ranks.

The noH input has 495 columns (205 N + 288 R + 2 flags). The H input has 500 (+ 5 H).

4. Fellow eye at the same visit

For each scan, the fellow is the other eye's examination on the same calendar day. It is found in the full raw Pentacam export of the clinic, about 11,760 exams. When several exams exist, the one with status "OK" is preferred, then the one with the fewest missing numerics. The fellow is chosen without reading any label, CXL or keratoplasty field. A fellow that was already treated contributes its measured numerics like any other.

  • Fellow numerics: available for 941 of 944 scans.
  • Fellow map image: a same-day four-map composite of the other eye exists for 563 of 944 scans. Where it is missing, the fellow R columns are missing (§10).

5. Block N: bilateral numerics (205 columns)

Per eye, N uses the scanner's 54 numerics plus age, and 11 engineered keratoconus indices. Each is a pure function of one scan:

kmax_minus_km        = KMax − Km F
pachy_min_over_apex  = Pachy Min / Pachy Apex
pachy_min_over_pupil = Pachy Min / Pachy Pupil
pachy_min_per_age    = Pachy Min / age
prog_mean, prog_max  = mean / max of the D*mm pachymetric progression indices
pachy_drop_4mm       = D0mm Pachy − D4mm Pachy
ecc_front_back       = Num. Ecc. F − Num. Ecc. B
asph_ratio           = Asph. Q F / Asph. Q B
elev_ratio           = Rf F / Rf B
cvol_ratio           = C.Vol D 3mm / C.Vol D 10mm
partcolumnscontent
own66age, 54 numerics and 11 indices of the scanned eye
fel65the same 65 non-age values for the fellow eye
asym65own − fellow
worse_*81 if the scanned eye is worse than its fellow: steeper KMax, steeper K2, higher ISV, KI, IHD, IVA or prog_max; thinner Pachy Min
has_fellow1whether a same-day fellow exam exists

In the LightGBM member, age is constrained to be monotone decreasing: younger patients are referred more often.

6. Map decoding: colour maps to physical values

Each examination exports a "4 Maps Selectable" composite with four colour-coded maps on absolute scales: corneal thickness, sagittal front curvature, front elevation and back elevation (both relative to the best-fit sphere). Each map carries its own 31-step colour bar. The decoder samples the bar of every image, then assigns each map pixel the value of the nearest palette colour. Pixels far from every palette colour (text, symbols, axis marks) become missing.

mapscale
thickness T300 + 20k µm, k = 0…30
sagittal front curvature Klabelled steps 60, 50, 48 … 17.5 D, with midpoints between them
front / back elevation EF, EB−75 + 5k µm vs BFS

The map boxes are those of preprocessing/3_improved_crop_script.py. Values are 2×2-pixel block medians on a 145 × 145 grid, about 0.069 mm per cell (28.9 px per mm), centred on the corneal vertex. Left eyes are mirrored, so +x is nasal and +y is superior for both eyes. Decoding reads no labels.

7. Block R: map measurements (96 per eye → 288 columns)

R is computed from the decoded maps with fixed, label-free formulas, within the central 8 mm (radius ≤ 4 mm). Rings and quadrants are on the mirrored grid. Quadrants: superior 45°–135°, inferior −135° to −45°, nasal |θ| ≤ 45°, temporal |θ| > 135°.

groupcountdefinition
ring × quadrant means644 maps × rings 0–1, 1–2, 2–3, 3–4 mm × 4 quadrants
extremum164 maps × (value, x, y, distance from centre) of the thinnest point (T) or the maximum (K, EF, EB) within 4 mm
asymmetry84 maps × (inferior − superior, nasal − temporal) mean on the 2–4 mm annulus
dispersion44 maps × standard deviation within 3 mm
thickness progression4mean thickness on 1, 2 and 3 mm rings (±0.25 mm) around the thinnest point minus the thinnest value; the 3 mm / 1 mm ratio

A region is set to missing when more than 30% of its cells are undecodable. The full dictionary is in the Appendix.

Bilateral use. Inside each training fold, the 96 measurements are standardised with mean and SD fitted on the training rows' own maps. No PCA is applied. The fellow's maps (mirrored the same way) are transformed with the same scaler. Three copies enter the model: R_own*, R_fel* and R_asym* = own − fellow. Two flags mark availability: own_image and has_fellow_image.

8. Block H: fellow-eye treatment history (H arm only, 5 columns)

The source is the clinic's treatment sheet, which gives per-eye CXL and keratoplasty dates. Only dates strictly before the visit date count: a fellow CXL on the visit day is "not yet treated".

columndefinition
fellow_cxl_before_visit1 if the other eye had CXL before the visit date
years_since_fellow_cxltime since that CXL, in years
fellow_kp_before_visit1 if the other eye had keratoplasty before the visit date
fellow_cxl_unknownthe CXL cell is filled but has no parsable date. It is never treated as past.
fellow_tx_missingthe patient is not in the sheet. The other columns are then missing.

In the cohort, 244 of 944 scans have a fellow eye cross-linked before the visit. Direction: a treated fellow lowers the predicted referral probability. This is consistent with clinical practice: a second eye is not referred by default just because the first was treated.

9. Training rows and weighting

  • Cohort rows of the training fold.
  • Extra labelled exams, used for training only and never scored. These are 1,487 additional single-visit exams (299 positive, 503 patients) from the raw export. They follow the cohort rules: complete numerics, Km F ≤ 55, CXL eyes only before their CXL date, nothing on or after keratoplasty. Each extra exam has its own same-day fellow. Extras from cohort patients (900 rows) are used only when that patient is in the training fold. Extras have no map images, so their R columns are missing and own_image = 0.
  • First-visit weight. Cohort first-visit scans get sample weight 2 in the final fit of every member. All other rows get weight 1.

10. In-fold preprocessing

Everything below is fitted on the training rows of the fold and then applied to the test rows:

  1. R standardisation (§7).
  2. Each column is clipped to the training rows' 0.5% and 99.5% quantiles.
  3. Missing values are filled with the training-row median of the clipped column. Any column with no observed value is filled with 0.

11. Learners and the blend

All hyper-parameters are chosen by 4-fold patient-grouped stratified inner CV inside the training fold, by mean inner AUC. The chosen setting is then refitted on all training rows with the sample weights of §9.

memberfixed settingstuned grid

Blend. Within each test fold, each member's predicted probabilities are converted to ranks divided by the number of test scans. The final score is the unweighted mean of the three. No weights are fitted. The members are complementary: two tree ensembles and one sparse linear model.

12. Evaluation protocol

  • Outer CV: 5-fold StratifiedGroupKFold grouped by patient, so both eyes and all visits of a patient stay in the same fold. Repeated 5 times with seeds 2026–2030.
  • Pooled score: each scan's out-of-fold score is averaged over the 5 repeats. The AUC is computed on these 944 averaged scores. The per-repeat AUC mean ± SD is also reported.
  • First-visit AUC: the same scores restricted to the earliest cohort scan of each eye ({n_first} eyes).
  • 95% CI: patient-cluster bootstrap, 1,000 resamples of patients.
  • Paired comparisons: the same patient bootstrap applied to the AUC difference of two models scored on the same scans.
  • No test-fold information reaches training: preprocessing, tuning, R scaling and the extra exams all respect the fold.

15. Feature-block ladder: where the gain comes from

The same blend, training rows, inner tuning and folds, refitted on nested column sets (E29, pre-declared). Only the columns change. L1 is the own eye only (age + 54 numerics + 11 indices, 66 columns), the information of the previous single-eye paper. L2 adds the fellow eye and own − fellow differences (block N, 205). L2b adds the own eye's maps to L1 instead (163). Final is N + R (+ H). The H arm adds block H to every rung. Paired Δ uses the same patient-cluster bootstrap on first-visit eyes. Causal statements about what each block adds come from this ladder, not from SHAP.

16. What the model uses (SHAP)

Out-of-fold SHAP from the 25 cross-validation fits per arm (XGBoost and LightGBM: exact TreeSHAP; elastic-net: exact linear SHAP). Each member's attribution is divided by the SD of its margin and the three are averaged, matching the equal-weight blend; each scan's value is averaged over the 5 repeats. The fits reproduce the logged OOF scores exactly. Summaries use first-visit eyes; map (R) attributions only count eyes whose map exists. Own, fellow and own − fellow columns are linear combinations of each other, so SHAP credit between them depends on the encoding. Panel a therefore groups by information source (own eye vs fellow-derived, numerics vs maps); panel b shows the encoded families.

20. Matched pairs: eyes the single-eye model cannot separate

Pre-declared rule (PROTOCOL.md, E29): each referred first-visit eye with both maps is paired with the nearest not-referred eye on standardised Km F and age, from the same stage A and a different patient. Each eye is used once. The table gives the fraction of pairs ordered correctly (referred eye scored higher) by the single-eye model L1 and by the final noH model, with a sign test on the discordant pairs. Scores are within-fold ranks averaged over repeats, so cross-fold comparisons are approximate. Case figures with individual maps are kept locally (publication_v2/local/) and are not part of this report.

21. Frozen model and scoring new scans

python -I kcref/model.py freeze refits both arms on all {n} cohort scans plus all extra exams, using the same recipe and inner CV (seed 2026). The output is kcref/frozen/final.pkl, which is not shared because it is fitted on patient data. model.predict(fz, D, idx) scores new rows. Ranks cannot be formed within a test fold for a single new scan. Instead, each member's score is mapped to its percentile among the frozen member's scores on the {n} cohort scans, and the three percentiles are averaged. The ordering matches the evaluated blend.

23. Notes for writing

  • Model selection. The final model was selected among 11 pre-declared learner and input configurations (E28), on the same cross-validated data it is reported on, without a held-out set. Report the number of configurations tried. Present the blend's gain over its LightGBM member as consistent but not significant (§13.2).
  • Rank pooling. Blend scores are within-fold ranks. The pooled AUC across folds is therefore slightly less directly comparable than per-repeat AUCs and paired deltas, which are the fair comparisons.
  • H and the label share a source. H and the label come from the same treatment sheet. Leakage is prevented by the strict "before the visit date" rule (§8). State this explicitly.
  • Missing maps in training. Extra exams have no maps: about 54% of the R values in the training rows are imputed and flagged by own_image = 0. All scored (cohort) scans have their own map.
  • Stage 4 of approach A holds only 5 scans, all negative, because of the Km F ≤ 55 inclusion filter. This is by design and is not a model failure.
  • Explainability. SHAP shows what the model uses, not what causes referral. Credit between own, fellow and own − fellow encodings is not identifiable; use the ladder (§15) for "the fellow eye adds X".
